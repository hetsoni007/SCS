When outreach stops working, the instinct is to rewrite the email. Not yet. Run this first.

These are the five things that broke mine, worst first, from an audit of {{threads}} of my own threads. Each ends with a one-line check. Open your sent folder and go through them in order. Fixing number five while number one is still broken is wasted effort.

## 1. Wrong targeting

The biggest cause by a distance. {{wrong_audience}} of my messages went to people who were never going to buy: recruiters, job posters, roles that were already filled, people whose bio said "no agencies".

The pattern was hiring posts. A company hiring looks like a signal, but the person behind the post is often a recruiter whose job is to fill a seat, not to hire a studio. I wasn't checking who was actually behind the post.

The fix is one question, asked before you write: can this person say yes to the work?

:::check
Open your last 20 sent threads and mark every recipient who couldn't personally say yes. That's your wrong-audience rate.
:::

## 2. Auto-reply traps

{{auto_replies}} of the "replies" in my threads were automatic: out-of-office notices, ticket confirmations, "we've received your message". For too long, they counted as engagement.

That's worse than it sounds. It inflates your reply rate, which is the number you use to decide whether anything is wrong. A bot confirming delivery isn't a person reading your email.

:::check
Search your replies for "out of office", "automatic reply" and "auto-reply". Subtract them and recalculate your reply rate.
:::

## 3. Generic follow-ups on warm threads

The most expensive mistake on the list, because it wastes the rarest thing you have: someone who already engaged.

A technical founder had told me something specific on a thread. I followed up with "just following up". He replied: "you didn't read our direction." A warm thread, spent on nothing.

A follow-up has one job, which is to add something. A new finding, a change on their site, a response to what they said. "Just following up", "bumping this" and "circling back" add nothing except proof that you didn't read the thread.

:::check
Read your last five follow-ups. If any of them could go to a different prospect unchanged, it's generic.
:::

## 4. Touching dead leads

{{rejections}} people told me no, in plain words. Some still got a third message from me. Some got a fourth. My tracking was scattered, so nothing marked those threads as closed.

That isn't persistence. To the person who said no, it reads as not listening, and they remember your name for the wrong reason.

What fixed it for me was structural, not a resolution: a [CRM where a third touch doesn't exist](/tools/outreach-crm/), and where a "no" closes the thread for good.

:::check
Search your inbox for "not interested" and "no thanks", then check whether you wrote to any of those people again.
:::

## 5. Deliverability

The quietest cause. My emails went out {{send_gap}} apart, from a mailbox with no warm-up. Hard bounces and spam flags stacked up, and each one made the next email a little more likely to land in spam.

You don't get a notification when this happens. You get a slow slide in how many people even see your email, which drags down every other number on this list without telling you why.

The unglamorous basics: verify addresses before you send, space sends by minutes rather than seconds, warm up a new mailbox gradually, and set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on your domain.

:::check
Find your bounce rate for the last month. If you can't, that's the first thing to fix.
:::

## Reading your results

Fix them in order. If number one is broken, nothing downstream matters. A perfect email to a recruiter is still an email to a recruiter.

If one through four check out and replies are still thin, then it's time to look at the copy, and [the Quest email](/articles/the-quest-email/) is a good place to start. The full story behind all five, with every number, is in [the audit](/articles/the-audit/).
