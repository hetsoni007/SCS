WordPress vs Custom Website in 2026: Which Should You Build?

One of these costs ₹80,000 and ships in a month. The other costs ₹8 lakh and takes a quarter. Picking wrong in either direction is expensive — answer five questions and get an honest recommendation from a team that builds both.

The question behind the question

"Should I use WordPress or build a custom website?" is really asking: how much of my problem is standard, and how much is unique? WordPress powers over 40% of the web because most business websites need the same things — pages, a blog, a contact form, maybe a store — and it does all of that superbly for a fraction of the cost of building from scratch. Custom development earns its price only when your product is the website: an app, a marketplace, a SaaS tool, something WordPress would have to be bent out of shape to do.

When WordPress is the right answer

  • Marketing and brand websites. Pages, blog, forms, SEO — this is what WordPress was built for, and non-technical teams can edit content without calling a developer.
  • Standard e-commerce. WooCommerce handles products, payments (including Indian gateways like Razorpay — see our gateway comparison), shipping and GST-compliant invoicing without custom code.
  • Content businesses. Publications, course sites, membership content — mature plugin ecosystems cover 95% of what you'd otherwise build.
  • Budget-conscious timelines. A WordPress build ships in weeks, not months, at a fraction of custom cost — see real India pricing.

When custom development is worth it

  • The website IS the product. A booking platform, a two-sided marketplace, a SaaS dashboard — anything where users log in and do things beyond reading and buying.
  • A mobile app is part of the plan. If you'll need iOS/Android apps sharing a backend, a custom API (our MERN stack work) serves both from day one.
  • Unusual integrations or workflows. When the plugin doesn't exist and would take longer to force than to build properly.
  • Performance at unusual scale. Most sites never hit WordPress's limits — but real-time features, heavy computation or very high concurrency are custom territory.

You get cheap, SEO-friendly content pages and purpose-built product engineering — each tool doing what it's best at. We build both sides, which is exactly why we'll tell you honestly which you need.

What about Wix, Shopify and the other builders?

The WordPress-vs-custom question often hides a third option: hosted builders. The honest read — Shopify is a genuine WooCommerce alternative for pure e-commerce, trading flexibility and ownership for convenience at a higher monthly cost. Wix and Squarespace suit tiny sites where budget is the only constraint, but you're renting, not owning: your content lives on their platform, migration out is painful, and technical SEO control is limited. WordPress sits in the sweet spot for most Indian SMBs precisely because you own everything — the code, the content, the hosting relationship — at builder-level cost.

How the decision changes with growth plans

A useful stress test: describe your business in three years. If the answer is "the same, but with more customers," WordPress scales fine — add pages, add products, upgrade hosting. If the answer involves an app, user accounts, a second revenue stream through the platform itself, or features you'd struggle to name a plugin for, build the custom backend now or plan the hybrid — because retrofitting product architecture onto a WordPress site later is the most expensive of all the paths.

The mistakes we see

A note on timing: this decision is cheapest to get right at the start, but it isn't irreversible. Businesses move from WordPress to custom as products emerge, and (more often than people admit) from over-built custom platforms back to WordPress when maintenance costs bite. If you're already on the wrong side of the line, migration is a solved problem — plan it, don't dread it.

Over-building: spending ₹8 lakh on a custom site that WordPress would have delivered for ₹80,000 — usually sold by someone who only builds custom. Under-building: forcing a marketplace into WooCommerce with 30 plugins until it collapses under its own weight — usually chosen to save money that gets spent three times over on rescue work. The quiz above is designed to catch both.

Frequently asked questions

Is WordPress good enough for a professional business website?

Yes — over 40% of the web runs on it, including major brands. Quality depends on who builds and configures it, not the platform itself.

Is WordPress cheaper than a custom website?

Substantially. In India, a professional WordPress site typically runs ₹30,000–₹2,00,000 versus ₹3,00,000–₹20,00,000+ for custom development. The gap widens further with ongoing maintenance.

Can WordPress handle e-commerce in India?

Yes — WooCommerce with an Indian payment gateway (Razorpay, PayU, Cashfree) handles catalogues, payments, shipping and GST invoicing well for standard stores. Very large or unusual stores may outgrow it.

When should I NOT use WordPress?

When the website is itself the product — user dashboards, marketplaces, SaaS tools, real-time features — or when a mobile app sharing the same backend is on the roadmap. That's custom (or hybrid) territory.

Can you build either one?

Yes — we ship both WordPress websites and custom MERN/React Native products, which is why our recommendation isn't biased toward one hammer.
